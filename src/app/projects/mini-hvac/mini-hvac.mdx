# Mini HVAC / Automatic Cooling System

## 1. Project Overview  

During my **AvenueE** program back on Sep, 2025, my teammates and I designed and built a small-scale heating, 
ventilation, and air conditioning system to better understand how temperature control and airflow work in cars.
For this project, our team designed a small **Automatic Cooling System / Mini HVAC** for a parked
vehicle. The idea is to keep the interior at a safe and comfortable temperature by automatically
turning on cooling when the cabin gets too hot, instead of making the driver suffer in a “baking”
car when they return.

We built a proof-of-concept prototype using an Arduino and basic electronic components, with the
long-term goal of integrating a similar system into real vehicles.

## 3. Problem and Motivation  

On hot days, the interior of a parked car can quickly become much hotter than the outside air.
This can lead to:

- Discomfort when people first get into the vehicle  
- Potential **heat-related health issues** in extreme conditions  
- A more frustrated and distracted driver  

In cold weather, people face the opposite problem: the interior is freezing, and they must wait
for the cabin to warm up before feeling comfortable. A smarter HVAC system can help in both
situations by conditioning the interior **before** the driver enters.

---

## 4. Proposed Solution  

Our solution is a **Mini HVAC / Automatic Cooling System** that monitors the vehicle’s interior
temperature and compares it to a **desired setpoint**:

- The driver chooses a comfortable temperature.
- If the cabin gets more than a certain amount above that setpoint, the system automatically
  turns on cooling (the fan).
- Once the temperature returns to the comfortable range, the fan turns off.

In a full vehicle implementation, the same logic could also control a heater in cold weather and
interface with the car’s existing HVAC system.

---

## 5. Prototype Implementation  

We built our prototype using an **Arduino Uno** and an electronics kit.

**Main hardware:**

- Arduino Uno (or compatible board)  
- Temperature sensor  
- Small DC fan (represents the vehicle’s A/C)  
- LCD display to show temperature and status  
- Breadboard, resistors, jumper wires  
- Power from a USB cable or battery  

**How the prototype works:**

1. The temperature sensor continuously measures the “cabin” temperature on the breadboard.  
2. The Arduino reads this value and compares it to a chosen setpoint.  
3. When the measured temperature becomes **too high**, the Arduino turns the **fan ON**.  
4. When the temperature cools down to the target range, the Arduino turns the **fan OFF**.  
5. The LCD display shows the current temperature and whether the fan is on or off, making the
   behavior easy to demonstrate.

You can imagine replacing the small fan with a real car blower and A/C compressor, and putting
the sensor in the actual vehicle interior.

---

## 6. Control Logic  

The system is a simple form of **closed-loop control**. It keeps checking the temperature and
reacts when it moves too far away from the comfort range.

A simplified version of the logic looks like this:

```text
loop:
  read currentTemperature

  if currentTemperature >= setTemperature + threshold:
    // Too hot – turn cooling on
    fan = ON

  else if currentTemperature <= setTemperature:
    // Back to target or below – turn cooling off
    fan = OFF

  // repeat forever

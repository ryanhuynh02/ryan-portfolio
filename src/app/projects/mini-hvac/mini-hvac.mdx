## 1. Background  
<br />

During my **AvenueE** program back on Sep, 2025, my teammates and I designed and built a small-scale
heating, ventilation, and air conditioning system to better understand how
temperature control and airflow work in cars.  
<br />

The goal was to create a system that can **monitor temperature**, make
**automatic decisions**, and control components like a **fan** or **heater**
based on the readings.

<br />

## 1. Project Overview  

For this project, our team designed an **Automatic Cooling System / Mini HVAC** for a parked vehicle.  
The idea is to keep the interior at a safe and comfortable temperature by automatically turning on
cooling when the cabin gets too hot, instead of making the driver suffer in a “baking” car when they return.   

We focused on a **proof-of-concept prototype** using an Arduino and basic electronic components,
with the long-term vision of integrating a similar system into real vehicles.

<br />

## 2. Problem & Motivation  

On hot days, the interior of a parked car can easily become **20°F hotter** than the outside air. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  
This can lead to:

- Discomfort when people first get into the vehicle  
- Potential **heat injury**, including heat exhaustion or even heat stroke in extreme cases  
- A less focused, more irritated driver, which can increase the risk of **distracted driving**  

In winter, drivers face the opposite issue: the interior is freezing cold, and they must wait for
the car to warm up before driving comfortably. A smarter HVAC system can address both extremes
by conditioning the interior **before** the driver enters. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}  

<br />

## 3. Proposed Solution  

Our solution is an **Automatic HVAC System** that monitors the vehicle’s interior temperature and
compares it to a **driver-selected setpoint**.  

- The driver chooses a comfortable temperature.  
- When nobody is operating the vehicle and the cabin temperature drifts more than **10°F away**
  from that setpoint, the system automatically turns on the climate control.  
- For hot conditions, the **A/C (cooling)** turns on until the cabin returns to the desired range.  
- In a full implementation, the same logic could also control a **heater** in cold weather.   

The long-term goal is to **integrate this logic into the car’s HVAC system and engine control**,
so the vehicle can briefly turn on the A/C when needed and shut off once the temperature is back
to a safe level.

<br />

## 4. Prototype Implementation  

For our prototype, we built a small **mini HVAC / automatic cooling system** using an  
**Arduino Uno kit**. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}  

**Hardware used:**

- Arduino Uno (Elegoo UNO R3 compatible board)  
- Breadboard  
- Temperature sensor / thermometer  
- DC fan (represents the vehicle’s A/C)  
- LCD display for showing temperature and status  
- USB power from a laptop and/or battery  

**How it works:**

1. The **temperature sensor** continuously measures the “cabin” temperature on the breadboard.  
2. The Arduino compares this reading with a chosen **set temperature**.  
3. When the measured temperature is **10°F above** the setpoint, the Arduino turns on the **fan**. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}  
4. Once the temperature drops back below the threshold, the fan turns off.  
5. The **LCD display** shows the current temperature and the cooling status (on/off), which makes
   the behavior easy to demonstrate during presentations.

You can imagine replacing the fan with the car’s A/C compressor and blower, and the breadboard
sensor with a cabin temperature sensor integrated into the dashboard.

## 2. System Overview  
<br />

The mini HVAC system is made up of three main parts:

- **Sensing** – A temperature sensor that continuously reads the room or box temperature.  
- **Control** – A microcontroller (e.g., Arduino) that compares the sensed value
  with the desired setpoint.  
- **Actuation** – A small DC fan and/or heater element that turns on or off to keep
  the temperature within a comfortable range.

## 3. Hardware & Software  
<br />

**Hardware (example):**

- Microcontroller (e.g., Arduino Uno)
- Temperature sensor (e.g., DHT11 / DS18B20 / thermistor)
- Small DC fan and/or heating element
- Power supply & breadboard
- Indicator LEDs and resistors
- Wires, jumpers, and housing/enclosure

**Software / Logic:**

- Reads the temperature at a fixed interval  
- Compares it with a **user-defined setpoint**  
- If temperature is above the limit → **fan turns on**  
- If temperature is below the limit → **heater turns on** (if included)  
- Otherwise, the system stays in an **idle / comfortable** state

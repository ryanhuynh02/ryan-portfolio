## 3. Project Overview  


<br />

The idea is to keep the interior at a safe and comfortable temperature by automatically
turning on cooling when the cabin gets too hot, instead of making the driver suffer in a “baking”
car when they return. We built a proof-of-concept prototype using an Arduino and basic electronic components, with the
long-term goal of integrating a similar system into real vehicles.

## 6. Problem and Motivation  
<br />

On hot days, the interior of a parked car can quickly become much hotter than the outside air.
This can lead to:
<br />

<ul className="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 marker:text-slate-600">
    <li>Discomfort when people first get into the vehicle.</li>
    <li>Potential **heat-related health issues** in extreme conditions.</li>
    <li>A more frustrated and distracted driver.</li>
</ul>
<br />

In cold weather, people face the opposite problem: the interior is freezing, and they must wait
for the cabin to warm up before feeling comfortable. A smarter HVAC system can help in both
situations by conditioning the interior **before** the driver enters.
<br />

---

## 7. Proposed Solution  
<br />

Our solution is a **Mini HVAC / Automatic Cooling System** that monitors the vehicle’s interior
temperature and compares it to a **desired setpoint**:
<br />

<ul className="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 marker:text-slate-600">
    <li>The driver chooses a comfortable temperature.</li>
    <li>If the cabin gets more than a certain amount above that setpoint, the system automatically
        turns on cooling (the fan).</li>
    <li>Once the temperature returns to the comfortable range, the fan turns off.</li>
</ul>
<br />

In a full vehicle implementation, the same logic could also control a heater in cold weather and
interface with the car’s existing HVAC system.
<br />

---

## 8. Prototype Implementation  
<br />

We built our prototype using an **Arduino Uno** and an electronics kit.

**Main hardware:**
<br />

<ul className="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 marker:text-slate-600">
    <li>Arduino Uno (or compatible board)</li>
    <li>Temperature sensor</li>
    <li>Small DC fan (represents the vehicle’s A/C)</li>
    <li>LCD display to show temperature and status</li>
    <li>Breadboard, resistors, jumper wires</li>
    <li>Power from a USB cable or battery</li>
</ul>
<br />

**How the prototype works:**
<br />

**1.** The temperature sensor continuously measures the “cabin” temperature.  
**2.** The Arduino reads this value and compares it to a setpoint.  
**3.** When it’s too hot, the fan turns ON.  
**4.** When it cools down, the fan turns OFF.  
**5.** The LCD shows the temperature and status.
<br />

You can imagine replacing the small fan with a real car blower and A/C compressor, and putting
the sensor in the actual vehicle interior.
<br />

## 9. Control Logic  
<br />

The system is a simple form of **closed-loop control**. It keeps checking the temperature and
reacts when it moves too far away from the comfort range.
<br />

A simplified version of the logic looks like this:
<br />

```text
loop:
  read currentTemperature

  if currentTemperature >= setTemperature + threshold:
    // Too hot – turn cooling on
    fan = ON

  else if currentTemperature <= setTemperature:
    // Back to target or below – turn cooling off
    fan = OFF

  // repeat forever

## Check-in & Safety Brief

I arrived at the pickup point, scanned in, and got a short safety overview. The operator explained how the system hands off to manual control in edge cases and how the redundant sensors (LiDAR, radar, and cameras) overlap to give the vehicle 360° awareness.

## The Ride

Acceleration was gentle, lane-keeping precise, and the robotaxi left more buffer than most human drivers. Lane changes were signaled early and executed with a steady arc—no jerks. At four-way stops, it yielded predictably, then committed cleanly when it was its turn.

## What Stood Out

- **Consistency over raw speed.** It never rushed yellows or tailgated.
- **Merge behavior** felt human-aware—signaled, matched speed, and slotted in without cutting anyone off.
- **Edge-case caution.** It paused for a pedestrian who looked undecided—good call that kept everyone comfortable.
- **Comfort tuning.** Starts/stops were feathered to avoid head-bob; passengers could talk or use their phones without bracing.

## Final Thoughts

As a computer-engineering student, the most impressive part was the **system integration**: perception, prediction, and planning all working in real time. It isn’t flashy; it’s steady—and that’s exactly what you want from autonomy. I left more optimistic about everyday robotaxi reliability, especially for predictable urban routes.

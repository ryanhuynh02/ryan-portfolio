## Check-in & Safety Brief
I arrived at the pickup point, scanned in, and got a short safety overview. The operator explained how the system hands off to manual control in edge cases and how the redundant sensors (LiDAR, radar, and cameras) overlap.

<ImgCap src="/blog/zoox/lidar-array.jpg" alt="Sensor array" cap="The roofline sensor suite that powers 360° perception." />

## The Ride
Acceleration was gentle, lane-keeping precise, and the robotaxi left more buffer than most human drivers. Lane changes were signaled early and executed with a steady arc—no jerks.

<ImgCap src="/blog/zoox/intersection.jpg" alt="Intersection behavior" cap="Handled an unprotected left with a clear gap selection, then a smooth commit." />

## What Stood Out
- **Consistency > raw speed.** It never rushed yellow lights or tailgated.
- **Merge behavior** felt human-aware—signaled, matched speed, and slotted in.
- **Edge-case caution.** It paused for a pedestrian who looked undecided—good call.

## Final Thoughts
As a computer-engineering student, the most impressive part is the **system integration**: perception, prediction, and planning all working in real time. It isn’t flashy; it’s steady—and that’s exactly what you want from autonomy.
